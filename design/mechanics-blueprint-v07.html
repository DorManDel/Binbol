Project vision

Why BinBol exists

BinBol studies when semantic meaning, short binary identifiers, shared dictionaries and visual glyphs can make selected workloads easier to author, communicate or store. The actual encoded bytes and their overhead are always measured separately from the visual number of symbols.

Three separate layers

Semantic dictionary / meaning↔Binary IDs and operands↔Glyph renderer

Three lab tracks

Action: Symbolic instruction and restricted educational VM. Dictionary: Text or recipes exchanged with shared/embedded registries. Glyph Forge: Shape, color and readability experiments. These are related ideas, but they do not automatically share one packet format.

What is verified and what is not

Our 16-bit VM has real encode/decode and arithmetic; disk and RAM are conceptual illustrations. A 200-byte repeated-text benchmark measured 23 bytes for a pre-shared BNB1 message, excluding initial dictionary distribution; 52 bytes with an embedded dictionary. No real network is used. A visual glyph does not guarantee byte savings or encryption.

Design decision pending

Should the primary story be a symbolic instruction, a shared message, or a guided route that begins with one and then opens the other? The current template allows testing both without replacing the architecture.
